What latent reasoning is

One line of the decoding loop, deleted.

The decoding loop

Fix a decoder-only language model. Write $f$ for the forward pass that maps a sequence of input embeddings to the final-layer hidden state at the last position, $W$ for the unembedding matrix, and $E$ for the embedding table. Autoregressive decoding is the loop

$$h_t = f(e_1, \dots, e_t), \qquad x_t \sim \mathrm{softmax}(W h_t), \qquad e_{t+1} = E[x_t].$$

The middle step is a quantiser. Compose it: the map $q = E \circ \mathrm{sample} \circ \mathrm{softmax} \circ W$ sends $\mathbb{R}^d$ to $\mathbb{R}^d$, but its image is the finite set of $|V|$ rows of the embedding table. A hidden state carries $d$ real coordinates; a token carries $\log_2 |V| \approx 17$ bits. Whatever the model computed at step $t$ that is not recoverable from $x_t$ does not survive to step $t+1$.

Latent reasoning is what happens when you delete $q$:

$$e_{t+1} = h_t.$$

That is the entire mechanical content of the continuous-thought family. The model keeps running its own forward pass on its own output, as before, but the output is now the unrounded state. Everything else — the architecture, the attention, the KV cache — is untouched. What changes is the width of the channel between one step of reasoning and the next.

Why the transcript is the whole story, until it isn't

It is worth being careful about a tempting objection: surely information also flows forward through the KV cache, which is continuous? It does not, in the following precise sense. The keys and values at position $t$ are a deterministic function of $e_1, \dots, e_t$, which are a deterministic function of $x_1, \dots, x_t$. Under token-space decoding, then:

The complete internal state of the computation at step $t$ is a deterministic function of the emitted token prefix. The transcript is a sufficient statistic for the computation.

This is the property that makes a chain of thought legible in principle, and it is exactly the property that latent reasoning gives up. Once $e_{t+1} = h_t$, the state at step $t+1$ is no longer a function of anything that was written down, and no amount of reading the output recovers it.

Two cautions, in both directions. The sufficient-statistic property is about the channel between steps, not about what happens inside one. A single forward pass computes a great deal that never appears in the transcript, which is why a chain of thought can be unfaithful to the computation that produced the answer even when it is the only channel available — see monitorability. Conversely, the property does real work: a model that wants to carry a hidden intermediate result across many serial steps has, in token space, about seventeen bits per step to do it in, and must otherwise re-derive the result inside each pass. The strength of chain-of-thought monitoring rests on that bottleneck, not on any assumption that models are honest.

Two axes

Methods in this literature vary along two independent axes, and most confusion about the field comes from conflating them.

Where the loop closes. A reasoning step can be a new sequence position — the loop runs around the outside of the forward pass, one token of context consumed per step — or it can be a new pass through the same layers, the loop running inside the stack with no sequence position spent. The first buys serial computation at the cost of context length; the second buys serial depth at the cost of latency, and is where recurrent-depth architectures live.

What survives the loop. A token, a continuous vector, or nothing at all. The third case is not a joke: filler and pause tokens add sequence positions and compute while passing essentially no task content through the channel, which makes them the cleanest experiment anyone has run on what intermediate tokens are actually for.

The two shaded cells are what the field means by latent reasoning. The literature on the unshaded cells is what tells us whether it should work.

Why anyone expects this to help

Three arguments recur, and they are not the same argument.

Bandwidth. A reasoning step that must be rounded to a token cannot carry a probability distribution, a partial ordering, or a superposition of candidate answers to the next step; it must commit. A continuous state can carry all of them, so search that would require backtracking in token space can in principle proceed in parallel. This is the argument that continuous thoughts encode a frontier rather than a path, and it is the one with the most direct empirical support.

Expressivity. Serial steps add computational power to a transformer in a way that width and parameters do not. The theory here is about token-space steps and is genuinely settled in outline; whether continuous steps add strictly more is a separate and much less settled question. See theory.

Cost. Natural language is a wasteful encoding of intermediate arithmetic. If the same computation can be done in fewer, denser steps, inference gets cheaper. This is the argument that motivates most of the compression work, and it is the one where claimed gains are most sensitive to how the baseline was tuned.
