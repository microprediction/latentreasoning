Implementations

What you can actually run. Repository statistics verified September 2026.

The method-specific repositories are research code: self-contained academic releases at GPT-2 to 8B scale, without packaging and frequently pinned to a stale dependency snapshot. Most reproduce their paper's numbers. None is something to add to a serving stack.

The open weights are a different matter, and the gap between the two has widened. Ouro and Nanbeige4.2-3B both load through standard tooling, and the latter was trained on 28T tokens and shipped as a general agentic model rather than as a demonstration. Depth recurrence has left the lab even though the methods around it have not.

Open model checkpoints

Three sets of open weights reason by depth recurrence.

ModelScaleNotesLicense
Ouro 1.4B / 2.6B
7.7T tokens		 Looped language model, 24 layers, 4 recurrent steps by default with configurable early exit. Instruction-tuned -Thinking variants published alongside. Loads through standard Hugging Face tooling. Claims 3–4B dense-equivalent quality at 2.6B parameters. The only release here a practitioner could plausibly deploy rather than study. Apache-2.0
Nanbeige4.2-3B 3B non-embed
28T tokens		 A general agentic model built on a looped transformer that reuses its layer stack for extra depth without extra parameters, trained on 28T tokens and released with open weights. Roughly thirty-five times the training tokens of Huginn and about four times Ouro, and the first case here of a looped architecture shipped as a general-purpose product rather than as a research artefact. An independent deployment report documents five bugs breaking the released checkpoint under Hugging Face Transformers on Apple Silicon, including a silently zeroed RoPE buffer. see repo
Huginn-0125 3.5B
800B tokens		 Prelude / recurrent core / coda, with user-controlled test-time recursion (num_steps, typically 4–64) and entropy, KL and argmax-stability early-exit criteria. Eight intermediate training checkpoints also published. Explicitly a proof of concept and undertrained relative to modern dense models of the same size. Apache-2.0

Papers with no public implementation

  • Pause tokens (Goyal et al., arXiv:2310.02226): never released by Google Research. The only related public repo is an independent toy that predates the paper and does not reproduce its pretraining setup.
  • Compressed Chain of Thought (Cheng & Van Durme, arXiv:2412.13171): no release found anywhere. An unrelated multimodal-prompting repository shares the CCoT acronym.
  • CoTFormer (arXiv:2310.10845): no official repository, only a later independent reproduction paper.
  • Loop the Loopies! (arXiv:2607.16051): the largest looped model reported by total parameters, at 20B total and 2B active, so smaller than Huginn's 3.5B dense by active count. No public code or checkpoint.

Benchmarks and data

Three instruments were built for this question, and they measure different objects.

InstrumentWhat it measures
ProsQA
arXiv:2412.06769		 Search and planning on a synthetic DAG task, used to compare latent-CoT methods against each other. Roughly 17.9k train and 300 validation examples at 3–6 hops, shipped as JSON inside the Coconut repository rather than as an independent dataset.
Hagendorff & Fabi
arXiv:2504.10615		 Model-internal inference forced before the first answer token: the answer is signalled by the language of the initial response token, so it cannot be written out. 4,000 items, 18 models. The authors state they cannot rule out heuristic exploitation.
Think Fast
arXiv:2606.07157		 Breadth and scaling of no-CoT capacity: over 30,000 questions across 43 benchmarks, with no-CoT behaviour enforced, estimating a 50% task-completion time horizon against human times. A suite rather than a dataset, and its collated datasets are available on request rather than released.

Everything else is borrowed: ProntoQA, GSM8K-Aug (385k augmented samples with intermediate expressions stripped, now the de facto training corpus for implicit CoT), GSM-Hard, and the standard suites MATH-500, AIME, SVAMP, BIG-Bench Hard, CommonsenseQA, ARC-C and GPQA.

What is still missing is a shared instrument that discriminates between latent-reasoning architectures and rules out shortcut exploitation. The three above measure different things, none has been adopted as a community standard, and critique papers continue to construct bespoke diagnostics rather than reach for a common one. Cross-paper comparison remains correspondingly unreliable.

Framework support

No cross-framework API was found for generic continuous-thought decoding, pause tokens, user-controlled recurrent depth, or inspection of intermediate loop states. Huginn and Ouro ship custom modeling files loaded through trust_remote_code=True, which is a packaging convenience rather than a library feature.

One architecture did get native support, and its lifecycle is more informative than the absence would be. vLLM merged OuroForCausalLM on 30 October 2025, a 521-line implementation containing the recurrence loop itself rather than deferring to the repository's custom code, with Ouro-1.4B and 2.6B listed in the supported-model table. vLLM removed it on 25 July 2026, giving three reasons: the model was about a year old and superseded by Plamo3, it saw “very little usage in vLLM,” and it had been marked for deletion.

A major serving engine integrated a looped architecture and then withdrew it for lack of demand. That is a sharper reading of ecosystem maturity than never having been integrated at all. What the integration was not also matters: inference support for one architecture, not a general interface for latent reasoning.
