Remind Me Again Why Large Language Models Can't Think

Peter Cotton, 6 April 2023 · The Modern Scientist · retrospective written September 2026, 3.4 years on

The argument

Written four months after ChatGPT's release, the essay stages the reductionist case against machine reasoning and declines to accept it on the available evidence. It works through the standard objections of the period in turn: that reasoning cannot be learned from text alone without sensory grounding, that pattern-matching is not understanding, that deep networks cannot represent relations and propositions precisely, and that hallucination betrays the absence of a world model. Marcus, LeCun, Mitchell, Davis and Edelman supply the prosecution.

The essay turns on a model of what language is evidence of. Thought is treated as structure in some internal representation space, and speech as the output of a composition of observation maps applied to that structure — lossy, partial, and in the essay's phrase the “wrong” format. Recovering the internal state from the words becomes an inverse problem, and the essay notes that a model need not arrive at the same internal state to be doing something real: a different set of representations “conspiring to create mostly similar observations, predictions and conclusions” would do. Human utterances are then valuable less as facts than as traces of the machinery, in the way cloud-chamber tracks are valuable.

What held up

The observation-map framing is the one this field adopted. Treating the transcript as a lossy observation of an internal state, rather than as the computation itself, is the assumption underneath every method on this site. The introduction states it as the distinction between the discretised feedback path and the retained activations. The essay got there before there was a literature to get there with.

Internalised speech became a training method. The essay invokes the developmental picture in which speech is internalised and becomes inner speech that still guides behaviour. Deng, Choi and Shieber's stepwise internalisation does precisely this by construction, deleting the explicit rationale on a schedule until the same forward pass performs the work unaided. What was an analogy in 2023 is a training curriculum with benchmark numbers attached.

Reasoning does happen where the words are not. The essay's insistence that the interesting structure is non-verbal, and that nobody could say whether a concept had been learned by reading outputs, anticipated the faithfulness results. Models resolve intermediate entities internally without verbalising them, plan several tokens ahead, and verbalise a hint they demonstrably used well under a fifth of the time. Monitorability collects the evidence.

Language does betray the representations underneath. The essay declines to accept what it calls the cloaking argument, holding it “underdeveloped at best” and doubting that deep representations could never be betrayed by language alone. The decoding work since then runs that way: intermediate values are recoverable from hidden states at filler positions with 80–95% accuracy, and latent traces decode to a verified natural-language trace 65–93% of the time where they carry the computation. Neither result reads thought off utterances, which is the stronger claim the essay was arguing against, but both cut against principled inaccessibility.

The grounding objection did fade. The essay predicts that text-only models form much the same representations as multimodal ones, and that gaps could be closed after the fact. Multimodal training arrived and the grounding argument largely stopped being the live objection.

What it got wrong, or could not have known

The question it argues about was dissolved rather than settled. The essay contests whether language models think. The field stopped asking. The 2026 questions are where the computation sits, how many bits pass between steps, and whether any of it can be read — none of which require adjudicating thought. The essay's own framing contributed to that dissolution, which is a strange way to lose an argument.

Some of the mystery was formalised away. The essay treats the limits of a forward pass as open. Within two years they were characterised: a log-precision transformer in a single pass sits inside uniform $\mathsf{TC}^0$, and serial steps convert token count into circuit size. Those results give a precise account of what one pass cannot do and what extra steps buy, where the essay had an analogy about filtering. The theory page states the bounds.

It did not anticipate the detour through explicit text. The essay's emphasis is internal representation. What followed instead was three years in which the dominant method for improving reasoning was making models write vastly more of it down, at length, in natural language. Latent reasoning is in part a reaction to the cost of that detour. A 2023 reader of this essay would have been surprised by 2025.

Net

Prescient on mechanism, superseded on framing. Thought as internal structure imperfectly observed through language is the picture the field settled into. Its central question — whether the machine thinks — is one the subsequent literature routed around entirely.
