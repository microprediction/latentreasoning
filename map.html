The gaps, spelled out

Dashed edges are claims about the literature, so they are stated here in full with a confidence. An absence is only interesting if someone actually looked.

  1. Interpretability results have not reached the deployment question. The finding that latent traces decode 65–93% of the time and the live controversy over a production recurrent-depth model do not cite each other. Nobody has applied the decoding methods to the deployed case. Confidence: high. The most policy-relevant gap here.
  2. No independent measurement of monitorability loss for a recurrent-depth model. A measurement suite for monitorability exists, and open recurrent-depth checkpoints exist. Nobody has run the former on the latter. Confidence: high.
  3. No purpose-built benchmark isolates genuine latent computation. ProsQA is the only benchmark built for this field and is small, synthetic and shipped inside one repository. Every critique paper has had to construct its own diagnostic rather than reach for a shared instrument. Confidence: high.
  4. No theory of credit assignment through recurrent latent states. Standard policy-gradient objectives implicitly assume single-step decisions; the observation that this mis-specifies reward for multi-step latent computation is recent and not broadly replicated. There is nothing here resembling the credit-assignment literature for token-level RLHF. Confidence: medium-high, and likely to close within a year.
  5. The two theoretical toolkits have no known reduction between them. Contentless tokens are analysed with finite model theory and quantifier depth; continuous thoughts with circuit-depth iteration and superposition arguments. They describe the same phenomenon from two sides and do not meet. Confidence: high.
  6. No scaling law for recurrent depth. Nothing fits loss jointly against parameters, loop count and tokens, so there is no compute-optimal allocation between widening the block and looping it more. Individual papers report compute-equivalent curves; none generalises. Confidence: high.
  7. Weights-as-memory and cache-as-scratchpad have never been compared head to head. Test-time training compresses context into weights; KV-cache distillation compresses it into the cache. These are competing answers to where state lives during long-horizon reasoning, and no paper evaluates them against each other on reasoning tasks. Confidence: medium.
  8. State-space recurrence is not being used as the reasoning medium. Essentially all of this work loops attention blocks. Treating a linear-recurrent state as the latent reasoning substrate is barely explored, despite the efficiency properties that motivated looping in the first place. Confidence: medium.
  9. Latent reasoning in diffusion language models has not met verifiable-reward RL. Both subfields are active; no work found combines them. Confidence: medium — both areas move fast enough that this may already be closing.
